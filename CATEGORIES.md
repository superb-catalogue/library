# The shelf's categories

Every book here carries a plain category, so someone traversing the library
can sort by what a book actually *is* rather than by which bundle it arrived
in.

## Where they come from

Standard Ebooks already tags every edition with a genre of its own — one
plain word or phrase describing the kind of book it is: Fiction, Poetry,
Horror, and so on. That is a different thing from their *collections*
(prize lists like the Pulitzer winners, publisher canons like the Harvard
Classics, series like Sherlock Holmes). Collections describe how a book was
bundled for a particular list; genre describes what the book is. Someone
browsing a shelf thinks "a mystery" long before they think "a James Tait
Black Memorial Prize winner" — so the shelf is sorted on genre, and
collection membership is kept as extra information on each book rather than
used to sort it.

## The categories

Sixteen raw genre values turned up across the archive, several of them rare
enough on their own (one Satire, four Science Fiction) that giving each its
own shelf would be a shelf of one book. They're folded into eleven:

- **Fiction**
- **Adventure**
- **Mystery & Horror**
- **Fantasy & Science Fiction**
- **Comedy & Satire**
- **Children's**
- **Drama**
- **Poetry**
- **Philosophy**
- **Nonfiction**
- **Biography & Memoir**

Nothing is thrown away in the folding — each book's own raw genre and its
collection memberships are both kept on its `provenance.json`. The category
is a sorting decision for the shelf, not a replacement for the record.

## Where this lives

`books/INDEX.json` is the machine-readable row-per-book source of truth,
generated by `scripts/ingest.py`. `LIBRARY.md` at the repository root is the
human-readable shelf, generated from that index — never edited by hand. A
check regenerates `LIBRARY.md` and fails if it drifts from what's committed,
so the two never go out of step with each other by accident.
