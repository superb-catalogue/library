# The shelf's categories

Every book here carries a plain category, so someone traversing the library
can sort by what a book actually *is* rather than by which bundle it arrived
in.

## Where they come from

Standard Ebooks already tags every edition with a genre of its own: one
plain word or phrase describing the kind of book it is, such as Fiction,
Poetry or Horror. That is a different thing from their *collections* (prize
lists like the Pulitzer winners, publisher canons like the Harvard Classics,
series like Sherlock Holmes). Collections describe how a book was bundled for
a particular list; genre describes what the book is. Someone browsing a shelf
thinks "a mystery" long before they think "a James Tait Black Memorial Prize
winner", so the shelf is sorted on genre, and collection membership is kept as
extra information on each book rather than used to sort it.

## The categories

Eighteen raw genre values turn up across the archive, several of them rare
enough on their own (three Spirituality, seven Biography, eight Shorts) that
giving each its own shelf would be a shelf of one book. They fold into eleven:

- **Fiction**
- **Adventure**
- **Mystery & Horror**
- **Fantasy & Science Fiction**
- **Comedy & Satire**
- **Children's**
- **Drama**
- **Poetry**
- **Philosophy**
- **Nonfiction**
- **Biography & Memoir**

Most of the foldings are obvious: Satire joins Comedy, Horror joins Mystery,
Autobiography and Memoir join Biography. Two are editorial, so they are
written down rather than left for someone to infer.

**Shorts** is a format rather than a subject. The eight books whose primary
genre it is are all short prose collections (Aesop, Bierce, Gibran, Lawson,
Maugham), and they shelve as Fiction, which is where someone looking for them
would go.

**Spirituality** covers three devotional works: Thomas à Kempis's *The
Imitation of Christ*, A. W. Tozer's *The Pursuit of God*, and Archibald
Alexander's *A Day at a Time*. They shelve as Philosophy, this library's shelf
for works of thought, rather than as Nonfiction, which here holds history,
science and essays.

Nothing is thrown away in the folding. Each book's own raw genre and its
collection memberships are both kept on its `provenance.json`. The category
is a sorting decision for the shelf, not a replacement for the record.

## Where this lives

`books/INDEX.json` is the machine-readable row-per-book source of truth,
generated by `scripts/ingest.py`. `LIBRARY.md` at the repository root is the
human-readable shelf, generated from that index and never edited by hand. A
check regenerates `LIBRARY.md` and fails if it drifts from what is committed,
so the two never go out of step with each other by accident.
